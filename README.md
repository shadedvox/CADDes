### Strandbeest (Theo Jansen Mechanism)

**Description:**  
A scaled-down mechanical walker modeled after Theo Jansen’s wind-powered kinetic sculptures. It utilizes an 11-bar linkage calculated using Jansen's "holy numbers" to transform a simple continuous rotational input into an organic, smooth walking stride.

### Hyperboloidal Gear Pair

**Description:**  
A specialized gear arrangement engineered for non-parallel, non-intersecting skewed shaft alignments. Pitch bodies follow hyperboloidal surfaces to maintain continuous line contact across skewed axes, effectively functioning as bevel gears for offset orientations.

### Bevel Gear Differential Mechanism

**Description:**

A functional automotive differential modeled in Fusion 360 using internal bevel gearing. It allows two coaxial output shafts to rotate at independent angular velocities while receiving torque from a single input shaft. Central spider gears mesh with side bevel gears inside a cylindrical housing to equalize torque distribution during uneven output rotation—mimicking real-world vehicle axle dynamics during cornering.

### Crossed Helical Gears

**Description:**  
Helical gearing adapted to transmit power between non-intersecting shafts via localized point contact. While offering lower load capabilities compared to intersecting bevel gears, they are significantly easier to model and manufacture—operating on the fundamental mechanics extended by worm drives.

### Scooper End-Effector

**Description:**  
A compact robotic arm attachment designed in Fusion 360 that replaces traditional gripper fingers with dual curved scoop jaws. Actuators drive the pivot arms relative to a rigid central support column, offering reliable capture and containment capabilities while keeping overall mass and bulk to a minimum. Features a universal top mounting bracket for direct integration with robotic arms.

### Non-Standard Oval Gears

**Description:**  
A non-circular gear set that converts uniform rotational speed into cyclic, variable output speed due to a continuously shifting pitch radius. The conjugate tooth profile was mathematically derived to eliminate pitch mesh gaps and binding issues encountered during standard geometric modeling.
